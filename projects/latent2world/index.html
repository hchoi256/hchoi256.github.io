Abstract

Pixel-space world models forecast future GUI screens before action selection, but this render-and-re-perceive paradigm is costly and requires reconstructing appearance details that are often unnecessary for deciding what to do next. We instead shift GUI world modeling from full-screen reconstruction to action-induced future information in latent space. A large multimodal model produces shared representations used by an action head for candidate scoring and a transition head for predictive future modeling. An action-aware contrastive objective further discourages the identity shortcut by separating the predicted next-state representation from the current state and unrelated transitions. Across offline and online GUI settings, the latent formulation improves action selection while substantially reducing the inference cost of pixel-space world models.

Why Latent World Modeling?

GUI transitions can vary dramatically in appearance, while explicit future-screen synthesis adds considerable latency. Latent2World focuses prediction on decision-relevant future consequences rather than pixel-perfect reconstruction.

Performance–efficiency comparison and GUI transition variability. The analysis motivates replacing expensive pixel-space generation with compact predictive latent modeling.

Decision-Relevant Prediction
Predict the future information needed to compare candidate actions rather than reconstructing every visual detail.
Action-Induced Change
Learn representations that encode what an action changes instead of preserving the current GUI state.
Efficient Action Selection
Use predictive supervision during training while retaining single-pass candidate rescoring at inference.

Latent World-Action Model

The same LMM representation is trained to support both action selection and action-conditioned future prediction.

Training jointly optimizes candidate-action scoring and contrastive future prediction. At inference, a confidence gate invokes latent world-model rescoring only when needed, and the transition head is not required for action scoring.

Core idea. Predictive future-state supervision directly shapes the representation used to score actions, bypassing the conventional render-and-re-perceive pipeline.

Mitigating the Identity Shortcut

Pixel-space predictors can stay close to the current screen even when the true next GUI changes substantially. Action-aware contrastive learning explicitly encourages action-specific future changes.

Identity-shortcut example and shortcut severity across weak, medium, and strong GUI transitions. The latent model reduces shortcut severity while maintaining stronger downstream success rates.
