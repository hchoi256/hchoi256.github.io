Abstract

Pixel-space world models have recently emerged as a promising approach for GUI agents, predicting future screens prior to execution and subsequently re-perceiving them for action selection. However, this two-stage paradigm faces two key challenges in GUI environments: the high computational cost of pixel reconstruction and the need to reconstruct large and diverse appearance-level details beyond what is required for action selection. In practice, this screen-generation objective can induce an identity shortcut problem, where future-state prediction largely preserves the current state rather than capturing action-induced changes. We reformulate GUI world modeling by shifting the prediction target from full-screen reconstruction to action-induced future information in latent space. A large multimodal model serves as the latent world model, whose shared representations support dedicated heads for action selection and predictive transition modeling. We further introduce an action-aware contrastive objective that encourages transition representations to capture discriminative action-induced changes. Across offline and online GUI settings, our method achieves up to 31.9% and 40.6% relative gains in success rate over pixel-space world models, respectively, while reducing inference latency by up to 98.9%.

Motivation

Existing GUI world models often follow a render-and-re-perceive pipeline: they explicitly reconstruct a future screen for each candidate action and then run a separate model over those predictions for action selection. This introduces substantial latency and forces the model to reproduce visual details that are not necessarily needed for deciding what action to take.

Key idea. Future prediction need not reproduce what the future looks like. Instead, a GUI world model can learn compact, decision-relevant latent representations of the consequences induced by candidate actions.

Latent World-Action Model

During training, a LoRA-adapted large multimodal model produces action-specific shared latent representations. An action head maps these representations to candidate-action scores, while a transition head predicts action-conditioned future representations. The transition branch is trained with next-state supervision and an action-aware contrastive objective. At inference time, the transition head is removed, enabling single-pass action scoring while retaining representations shaped by predictive future-state supervision.

Mitigating the Identity Shortcut

Pixel-space world models can preserve the current GUI even when the true next state changes substantially. The action-aware contrastive objective explicitly separates predicted future representations from the current-state representation and unrelated transition latents, encouraging action-specific future information. This substantially attenuates shortcut severity and maintains more stable downstream success rates as transition magnitude increases.

Online Results

On AndroidWorld, the latent world-action model improves Overall SR over the corresponding base agent across all five evaluated backbones. It also consistently outperforms the pixel-space world-model baselines while avoiding explicit future-screen generation.
