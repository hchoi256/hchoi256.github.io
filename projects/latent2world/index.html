Abstract

Pixel-space world models have recently emerged as a promising approach for GUI agents, predicting future screens prior to execution and subsequently re-perceiving them for action selection. However, this two-stage paradigm faces two key challenges in GUI environments: (L1) the high computational cost of pixel reconstruction and (L2) the need to reconstruct large and diverse appearance-level details beyond what is required for action selection. In practice, this screen-generation objective can induce an identity shortcut problem, where future-state prediction largely preserves the current state rather than capturing action-induced changes. In this work, we reformulate GUI world modeling by shifting the prediction target from full-screen reconstruction to action-induced future information in latent space. Concretely, we leverage a large multimodal model as the latent world model, whose shared representations support two dedicated heads for both action selection and predictive transition modeling. The action head directly translates these latent representations into action selection, while the transition head captures action-conditioned future information. To mitigate the identity shortcut, we further introduce an action-aware contrastive objective that encourages the transition representations to capture discriminative action-induced changes. Extensive experiments across offline and online GUI settings show that our method achieves up to 31.9% and 40.6% relative gains in success rate over pixel-space world models, respectively, while substantially reducing inference latency by up to 98.9%.

Why Latent World Modeling?

Existing GUI world models typically render a future screen and then perceive it again before choosing an action. This makes the world model solve a much harder problem than action selection actually requires: reconstructing the full visual future, including appearance details that may be irrelevant to deciding which candidate action is best.

Pixel-space prediction is both expensive and difficult in GUI environments. GUI transitions exhibit substantially broader visual variation than natural-video transitions, while pixel-space world models add large inference cost.

The key observation is that a GUI agent does not need to know exactly what every future pixel will look like. It mainly needs to know what each action will change and whether that consequence helps complete the task. Latent2World therefore moves the prediction target from full-screen reconstruction to compact, decision-relevant future information in latent space.

Avoid Full-Screen Reconstruction
Model action consequences directly instead of spending computation on rendering every future visual detail.
Focus on Action-Induced Change
Represent the information that distinguishes what candidate actions will cause, rather than simply preserving the current GUI.
Keep Prediction Decision-Relevant
Use future-state supervision to improve the representation used for action scoring, without requiring pixel generation at inference.

Latent World-Action Model

Latent2World uses a shared multimodal representation for two complementary objectives: selecting the right action and predicting the future consequence induced by that action. Predictive supervision therefore shapes the same representation that is ultimately used for decision making.

During training, the action head scores candidate actions while the transition head predicts an action-conditioned next-state latent. During inference, candidate rescoring is performed directly in latent space without generating a future screenshot.

Training
Joint action scoring + future prediction

Given the current screenshot, task goal, interaction history, and candidate actions, the LMM produces a shared hidden representation. The action head maps it to candidate scores, while the transition head predicts the latent representation of the next GUI state caused by the observed action.

The actual next screenshot supplies the positive future target. The current state and a memory bank of unrelated transitions act as negatives, so the model learns which future is specifically induced by the chosen action.

Inference
Single-pass latent rescoring

The GUI agent first proposes candidate actions with confidence. High-confidence decisions can be executed directly; when confidence is low, the latent world model re-scores the candidates using its predictive representation.

Crucially, the transition head is not needed for action scoring at inference. This removes the expensive render-and-re-perceive loop used by pixel-space world models.

Core idea. World modeling is used as predictive supervision for the action representation itself: the model learns what an action is likely to cause and directly uses that knowledge to decide which action to take.

Mitigating the Identity Shortcut

A future-state predictor can take an easy shortcut: keep representing the current screen even when the action should cause a large transition. In GUI environments this becomes especially problematic because a single click or navigation action can abruptly change most of the screen.

Pixel-space models increasingly preserve the current state as transition magnitude grows. Latent2World reduces shortcut severity and maintains substantially stronger downstream success on larger transitions.

Problem
Current-state information is an attractive shortcut

Point-wise future matching alone does not explicitly require the prediction to separate from the current state. As a result, the model can remain close to what it already sees instead of encoding the change introduced by the action.

Our Fix
Action-aware contrastive transition learning

The predicted future latent is pulled toward the true next-state latent, while the current-state latent is used as an explicit negative. Additional negatives from other transitions further force the representation to distinguish plausible but different action outcomes.

This objective makes future prediction about the change caused by the action, not simply about visual similarity to the current GUI. The effect is most visible on medium and strong transitions, where pixel-space shortcuts become more severe while the latent model remains more stable.

AndroidWorld

Compared with pixel-space world models, it retains predictive action evaluation while avoiding expensive future-screen generation.

Qualitative Analysis

Action-Specific Latent Structure

To test whether the predicted future representations actually encode action-induced changes rather than only current-screen appearance, we suppress shared current-state information and visualize the residual representations with t-SNE. The latent model forms much clearer Click, Scroll, and Type structures around their action centroids than the base agent and pixel-space world models.

Action-induced future representations after reducing current-state confounding. Latent (Ours) shows the clearest action-specific organization around the corresponding centroids.

Interpretation. The learned future representation is not merely a compressed copy of the current screen: it contains structure associated with how different actions change the GUI, which is the predictive information needed for downstream action selection.
